PRISMatic · PET recyclability decision tool

PRISM WP3 proof-of-concept demonstrator · PET reference system Illustrative model · placeholder data

Like a prism splitting light into its spectrum, PRISMatic separates a PET sample's spectral fingerprint into the decisions that follow — inferring degradation, predicting mechanical properties, and recommending mechanical, hybrid or chemical recycling.

PRISMatic previews the planned PRISM Python decision-support tool. It reads the spectral fingerprint of a post-consumer PET sample of unknown history and infers its degradation state, then weighs three transition criteria — technical (WP1), chemical (WP2) and sustainability (WP4) — to recommend whether the material should be mechanically recycled, chemically recycled, or handled by a hybrid route. The operator is never asked for the cycle number.

What's real vs emulated in this preview

Real / by design — carries into the deployed tool

  • The decision architecture: fingerprint → inferred state → fusion of three criteria (WP1/WP2/WP4) → route + confidence.
  • The physics-informed functional forms: exponential chain-scission decay, sigmoidal loss of mechanical properties, saturating oxidation growth.
  • The uncertainty logic: a spread on the inferred state that lowers decision stability near a route boundary.
  • The data model: PRISMatic's own sample-record structure (with an optional community-interoperability export).

Emulated / illustrative — placeholder until the real models exist

  • Every number — curve parameters, thresholds, crossover cycles, LCA/TEA values, yields — is a literature-scaled placeholder in one code block (PET), not a fitted or measured value.
  • The inference is an emulated PENN: a transparent stand-in with the same inputs and outputs as the planned physics-enforced neural network. It is not a trained network.
  • Decision-stability figures illustrate how uncertainty will be reported; they are not validated calibration.

When WP1/WP2/WP4 deliver fitted parameters and a trained PENN, they drop into this same interface — see the Roadmap and the Methodology & status drawer below.

  About PRISMatic · authorship and what PRISM contributes
  

    
Authorship

    
PRISMatic and the underlying PRISM framework were conceived and developed by
    Dr Oana M. Istrate and Prof. Peter Nockemann, Queen's University Belfast (2026).
    PRISM stands for Physics-Informed Recyclability Identification through Spectroscopic and Mechanical Modelling.
    First written record of the concept: 1 July 2026, Belfast.


    
What PRISM contributes

    
    
      
  • Decisions for material of unknown history. The degradation state is inferred from the spectral
      fingerprint (ATR-FTIR/NIR) alone; nobody needs to know how often the material has been reprocessed.
    • 
      
  • A mechanical↔chemical transition boundary. Technical (WP1), chemical (WP2) and sustainability (WP4)
      criteria are placed on one degradation axis and fused into a mechanical, hybrid or chemical recommendation,
      making explicit where mechanical recycling should give way to chemical recycling.
    • 
      
  • Physics-informed inference with honest uncertainty. Degradation physics constrains the inference
      (a physics-enforced neural network with a B-PINN-style band in the deployed tool), and the uncertainty is
      carried through to a decision-stability measure that flags samples near a route boundary.
    • 
      
  • From spectrum to mechanical performance. The same inferred state predicts the full mechanical
      profile — stress–strain behaviour, toughness, impact — through a spectral→mechanical reference library.
    • 
      
  • From decision to value. Each recommendation is linked to the end-use markets the material can
      still serve, with carbon and value estimates, so the trade-off between keeping material in mechanical
      loops and resetting it chemically is visible.
    • 
      
  • Transparent, reproducible decisions. Criterion weights are exposed and their influence is shown;
      every assessment is saved as a replayable record of inputs, versions and predictions.
    •

  Methodology & status · how this works and what's still placeholder
  


    
What this demonstrator computes

    
A spectral fingerprint (carbonyl index near 1715 cm⁻¹, carboxyl endgroups near 1685 cm⁻¹,
    and an NIR band-ratio proxy for intrinsic-viscosity / Mₙ loss) is mapped to an inferred degradation
    state expressed as a reprocessing-cycle equivalent. Three transition criteria are then evaluated at
    that state and fused into a weighted composite score that selects a route.


    
Physics-informed functional forms (the shapes the real models will fit)

    
    
      
  • Chain scission → exponential decay of intrinsic viscosity and Mₙ toward a floor.
    • 
      
  • Mechanical properties → sigmoidal loss of tensile strength and elongation at break (elongation collapses earliest).
    • 
      
  • Oxidation → saturating growth of carbonyl index and carboxyl endgroups.
    • 
      
  • Chemical recovery (WP2) → glycolysis rate rises as the chain shortens (the gentle yield rise used as the transition driver is an illustrative tendency); BHET purity falls with oxidation.
    • 
      
  • Sustainability (WP4) → per-functional-unit GWP and cost for mechanical vs chemical routes, with a crossover cycle.
    • 
    

    
These forms encode known degradation physics; only their parameters are placeholder.


    
The inference (emulated PENN)

    
A transparent stand-in inverts each marker through its functional form to a cycle estimate,
    then combines the three by weight; disagreement between markers plus a base measurement-noise term sets the
    uncertainty band. This mirrors the inputs, outputs and uncertainty behaviour of the planned
    physics-enforced neural network (PENN) with a B-PINN-style band — but it is a rule-based
    emulation, not a trained network. It exists so the interface, decision fusion and uncertainty
    reporting can be reviewed before training data is available.


    
Data model

    
PRISMatic holds the sample's history in its own internal transformation record
    (virgin PET → degraded state → glycolysis monomer BHET), using BigSMILES structures for the species.
    An optional export in the PolyDAT community format (Lin et al. 2021) supports data exchange.
    Operators never edit JSON.


    
Current status & limitations (read before drawing conclusions)

    
    
      
  • Every numeric parameter, threshold, crossover and impact figure is an illustrative literature-scaled placeholder, not a fitted or measured value.
    • 
      
  • The inference is emulated, not a trained PENN; confidence figures illustrate reporting, not validated calibration.
    • 
      
  • Scope is PET only. PA6 transfer is planned, not demonstrated.
    • 
      
  • No experimental spectra are used; example samples are synthetic draws around the placeholder curves, and the
      spectrum view is a synthetic sum-of-bands illustration of the slider values (real PET band positions, illustrative shapes).
    • 
      
  • When WP1/WP2/WP4 deliver fits and WP3 trains the PENN, they replace one code block and the inference function — the interface, criteria and record structure are unchanged by design.
    • 
    


    
    

    
Provenance of the placeholder numbers

    
    
      
  • Literature-scaled — parameter magnitudes chosen to sit in the range reported for PET degradation, glycolysis and LCA/TEA in the published literature (used to make curves realistic, not to reproduce any specific study).
    • 
      
  • Illustrative — thresholds, weights and fusion cut-offs set to give a clear, legible demonstration; these will be replaced by fitted / calibrated values.
    • 
    

    
No value on this page should be cited as a measured PET property.
